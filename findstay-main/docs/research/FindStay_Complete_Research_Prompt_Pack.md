# FindStay --- Project Research Prompt Pack

**Purpose:** A focused, evidence-based research pack for preparing the
academic documentation and first presentation of the FindStay
software-engineering project.

**Project name:** FindStay\
**Proposed geographical focus:** Ghana, with Takoradi as the suggested
initial local focus. Confirm this with the project team before making
location-specific claims.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# How to Use This Prompt Pack

Use the prompts in the order presented.

For each section:

1.  Start a new AI conversation or a fresh research task.
2.  Paste the **Project Context Prompt** first.
3.  Paste only the relevant section prompt immediately after it.
4.  Review the research and open the source links.
5.  Keep the research output and its references.
6.  Bring the findings back for critical review before adding them to
    the official project document.

The sections are intentionally separated so that research remains
focused and does not become repetitive or overly broad.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 0. Project Context Prompt

Paste this context before each section-specific prompt.

``` text
We are developing a school software-engineering project called FindStay.

FindStay is a two-sided accommodation platform intended initially for Ghana, with Takoradi as a proposed local focus. It helps guests find, compare, and reserve accommodation such as hostels, hotels, guesthouses, and apartments. It also provides property owners/managers with tools to manage listings, availability, reservations, income, expenses, occupancy, and reports. Administrators verify owners and listings, manage users, and handle complaints.

This is a proposed academic system, not an already deployed product. Do not invent survey results, statistics, interviews, laws, or features. When making factual claims, provide reliable, recent sources and direct links. Clearly distinguish sourced facts from reasonable project assumptions.

I need research material written in clear academic language that can later be adapted into a project document.

Use Ghana as the national context and Takoradi as the proposed local context where relevant evidence is available. Do not force local claims when reliable evidence cannot be found. Clearly identify when evidence relates to Ghana generally rather than Takoradi specifically.
```

# 1. Background of the Study

``` text
Using the FindStay project context above, research and draft a Background of the Study.

Explain the growth and importance of digital accommodation discovery and booking, the challenges faced by guests when finding trustworthy accommodation, and the operational problems property owners face when handling bookings, availability, income, and expenses manually or through disconnected tools.

Focus on the Ghana context, and Takoradi where reliable evidence is available, but do not force local claims without sources. End by explaining why a platform such as FindStay is relevant.

Provide:

1. A 500–700 word academic draft.
2. Key points in bullet form.
3. Every factual source as a direct link, with author/organisation and publication date where available.
4. A separate list of claims that need local research, interviews, or validation.
5. A clear distinction between established facts and reasonable project assumptions.

Do not invent statistics, interviews, or evidence. Avoid making unsupported claims about the absence of existing digital accommodation platforms.
```

# 2. Problem Statement

``` text
Using the FindStay project context above, identify the central problem the system intends to solve.

The problem statement must focus on real challenges, not simply say “there is no system.” Consider:

- Difficulty finding verified, suitable accommodation.
- Fragmented or unreliable information about prices, availability, and facilities.
- Manual booking processes and the risk of double booking.
- Difficulties for property owners in tracking bookings, revenue, expenses, and occupancy.
- Limited reporting and decision-making support.

Write:

1. One concise formal problem statement of 180–250 words.
2. A shorter presentation version of 70–100 words.
3. A list of specific problems mapped to the users affected: guests, property owners/managers, and administrators.
4. Sources or evidence supporting any factual claims.
5. A list of issues that should be validated through interviews or field research.

Do not claim FindStay is the first system of its kind. Do not present assumed problems as proven facts.
```

# 3. Project Aim and Measurable Objectives

``` text
Using the FindStay project context above, propose one clear project aim and 6–8 specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound-style objectives appropriate for a school software project.

The objectives should cover:

- User registration and role-based access.
- Accommodation discovery, filtering, and viewing details.
- Property listing and availability management.
- Reservation/booking management.
- Income and expense tracking.
- Reports or dashboards.
- Administration and property verification.
- Basic security and data protection.

Use action verbs such as design, develop, implement, enable, generate, and evaluate. Avoid objectives that are too broad, vague, or beyond a student project's scope.

Present the result in a table with:

- Objective number.
- Objective statement.
- Expected evidence of completion.

Ensure the objectives describe outcomes that can realistically be demonstrated and evaluated within the project's available time and resources. Do not include features that have not been agreed upon by the team.
```

# 4. Significance of the Project

``` text
Using the FindStay project context above, write the Significance of the Study/Project.

Explain the expected value of FindStay to:

- Guests or accommodation seekers.
- Hostel, hotel, guesthouse, and apartment owners/managers.
- Platform administrators.
- The academic institution/research community.
- The local tourism, accommodation, or digital-services ecosystem, where evidence supports this.

Keep the claims realistic. State benefits as expected contributions, not guaranteed outcomes.

Provide:

1. A 350–500 word academic draft.
2. A stakeholder-benefit table.
3. Sources for factual context.
4. A distinction between benefits directly supported by research and benefits anticipated from the proposed system.

Do not claim that the project will automatically increase revenue, eliminate fraud, or solve every accommodation-management problem.
```

# 5. Scope of the Project

``` text
Using the FindStay project context above, define a realistic Scope of the Project for an academic prototype or first version.

Separate the scope into:

1. In-scope users.
2. In-scope property types.
3. Guest/customer features.
4. Property-owner/manager features.
5. Administrator features.
6. Technical boundaries.
7. Explicitly out-of-scope features.

Keep the first version manageable. For example, do not assume live payment processing, mapping, AI recommendations, native mobile apps, or real-time third-party integrations unless they are intentionally included and feasible.

Clearly distinguish:

- Core features planned for the first version.
- Optional features that require further discussion.
- Future enhancements that are outside the current project scope.

Present the result in a structured academic format. Explain why important exclusions are outside the first version. Do not silently add features that the team has not approved.
```

# 6. Limitations and Assumptions

``` text
Using the FindStay project context above, propose realistic project limitations and assumptions for a school software project.

Limitations may include:

- Limited development time.
- Limited budget and development resources.
- Availability of test users and realistic test data.
- Restricted third-party integrations.
- Prototype-level security and performance testing.
- Limited geographical coverage.
- Difficulty obtaining cooperation or accurate information from accommodation providers.

Assumptions may include:

- Users have internet access and access to a suitable smartphone or computer.
- Owners provide accurate property and listing information.
- Property owners/managers update availability and booking information.
- Administrators review submitted listings and owner information.
- Users provide accurate registration and booking details.

Provide two separate tables:

**Limitations table**
- Limitation.
- Effect on the project.
- Mitigation where possible.

**Assumptions table**
- Assumption.
- Why it is necessary.
- Risk if it is untrue.

Do not present an assumption as a confirmed fact. Avoid listing normal design decisions as project limitations.
```

# 7. Existing-System Comparison

``` text
Compare existing accommodation marketplaces and property-management systems that are relevant to FindStay.

Include global examples such as Airbnb, Booking.com, Hostelworld, and suitable Ghanaian or locally relevant platforms. Include property-management tools where relevant.

Create a comparison table with:

- System name.
- Primary users.
- Key accommodation-search and booking features.
- Key owner-management features.
- Notable strengths.
- Verified limitations or gaps relevant to FindStay.
- Official website or reliable source.

Then identify a realistic gap FindStay could address: combining accommodation discovery and booking with accessible, lightweight property-management features for smaller accommodation providers in Ghana, particularly the proposed Takoradi market.

Research requirements:

1. Use official product documentation and websites wherever possible.
2. Use reliable independent sources when evaluating market context or user experience.
3. Do not make unsupported claims about competitors.
4. Do not describe a feature as missing unless a reliable source supports that conclusion.
5. Distinguish between a competitor's actual limitation and a feature that is simply outside the scope of FindStay.
6. Explain where FindStay may offer a different combination of features rather than claiming that its individual features are unique.

Conclude with a concise analysis of the opportunities and challenges for FindStay.
```

# 8. General Research Quality Checklist

Use this checklist when reviewing the output from each research task.

-   [ ] Does the research address the requested section directly?
-   [ ] Are factual claims supported by reliable sources?
-   [ ] Are direct source links included?
-   [ ] Are sources reasonably recent and authoritative?
-   [ ] Have Ghana-specific and Takoradi-specific claims been
    distinguished?
-   [ ] Are assumptions clearly labelled?
-   [ ] Have invented statistics, interviews, laws, and survey results
    been avoided?
-   [ ] Is the writing clear, formal, and academically appropriate?
-   [ ] Does the content avoid unnecessary repetition with other
    sections?
-   [ ] Is the proposed scope realistic for a student
    software-engineering project?
-   [ ] Are competitor comparisons fair and evidence-based?
-   [ ] Can the claims be defended if a lecturer asks for supporting
    evidence?

# 9. Final Review and Integration

After researching all seven sections, combine the reviewed material into
the project's Introduction chapter.

Before producing the final chapter:

1.  Check consistency in the project name, geographical focus,
    terminology, aim, objectives, and scope.
2.  Remove duplicated explanations.
3.  Resolve contradictions between the problem statement, objectives,
    and scope.
4.  Verify that every factual claim has an appropriate reference.
5.  Use one consistent academic referencing style as required by the
    institution.
6.  Ensure that the final chapter reads as one coherent academic
    document rather than seven unrelated AI-generated responses.
7.  Preserve the source list and supporting evidence for later
    presentation and assessment.

**Important:** The research outputs are working material. They should be
critically reviewed, verified, and adapted by the project team before
submission. Do not submit unverified AI-generated claims as established
research findings.
